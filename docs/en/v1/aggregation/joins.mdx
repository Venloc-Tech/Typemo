Data rarely lives in one collection. An order points to a customer, a category points to its parent, and old orders sit in an archive apart from the live ones. A normal read in MongoDB doesn't join collections, but aggregation can, in three ways.

- [`lookup`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#lookup) pulls documents from another collection into each row.
- [`unionWith`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#union-with) appends the documents of another collection to the result.
- [`graphLookup`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#graph-lookup) follows links however many there are, for example up a category tree.

Typemo knows the type of the pulled-in documents from the model you pass in `from`, and it checks the join fields against both models. A typo in `localField` or `foreignField` is a compile error, not an empty result in production.

## What you need [#prerequisites]

The client from [Connect to the database](../connection/connect.mdx) and the way to run an aggregation from [Your first aggregation](first-aggregation.mdx). The data is a small shop with four collections.

- Customers: `alice` from Germany and `bob` from France.
- Orders: two from `alice` (120 and 80), one from `bob` (50), and one from `dave` for 10. There is no customer `dave` in the database, and that will matter later.
- Order archive: one old order from `bob` for 300.
- Categories: `Electronics`, with `Phones` inside it and `Smartphones` inside `Phones`.

## Quick start [#quick-start]

To give every order its customer, name the collection in `from`, the order field in `localField`, the customer field in `foreignField`, and the new field in `as`.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/quick-start" twoslash />

The pulled-in field is always an **array**, because several customers might share that name, or none at all. If you want a single object, add [`unwind`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#unwind). We'll cover that and the other options below.

## Pull in documents by a matching field [#lookup-by-field]

Say the order report needs the customer's country. `lookup` finds the documents in `customers` whose `name` matches the order's `customer` and puts them in `buyer`. The row type grows with it, and the order fields gain an array of customers.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/lookup-by-field" twoslash />

Notice the order from `dave`. It's still there, but its `buyer` is empty. That's how the server works. `lookup` is a left join, so the rows on the left always stay. And the compiler won't let a typo like `localField: "custmer"` or `foreignField: "nam"` through.

Typemo takes the policies of the pulled-in collection from the class you pass in `from`. If `Customer` has soft delete turned on, deleted customers won't show up in `buyer`. If the collection is split by tenant, only the documents of the operation's tenant come in, and Typemo stops a query without a tenant before it reaches the server. `Hidden` fields are cut out too. You don't need to register a `Customer` model for any of this, since Typemo builds the schema from the class. [`unionWith`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#union-with) and [`graphLookup`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#graph-lookup) behave the same way.

## One object instead of an array [#single-object]

An array with one element is awkward to work with. The [`unwind`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#unwind) stage unwinds the array into one row per element, and the field becomes an object. Orders without a customer drop out, because there's nothing to unwind.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/single-object" twoslash />

After `unwind`, `f.buyer` is an object, and your editor suggests `f.buyer.country` on its own. To keep orders without a customer, write `unwind({ path: "$buyer", preserveNullAndEmptyArrays: true })`.

## Pull in with a condition [#lookup-with-condition]

Sometimes matching one field isn't enough. Suppose you want only a customer's large orders, and not the orders themselves but their total. For that, `lookup` has a form with a **sub-pipeline**. You pass values from the current row through `let` and build a separate pipeline over the collection you pull from. The variables inside it are typed the same way as `f`.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/lookup-with-condition" twoslash />

The first argument of the callback is a builder over `Order`, the second is your variables. The sub-pipeline decides the type of the pulled-in rows, so after `project` they hold only `total`. An empty sub-pipeline won't pass. It needs at least one stage, otherwise you get a `ConfigurationError`.

## Append documents from another collection [#union-with]

Sometimes you need one list out of two collections, for example live orders together with the archive. [`unionWith`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#union-with) appends every document of another collection to the rows. The row type becomes a **union** of the two types, and the compiler won't let you read `archivedAt` until you check that the row came from the archive.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/union-with" twoslash />

If you need only part of the second collection, or the same shape as the first, pass an object `{ coll, pipeline }`. The result then gets whatever the sub-pipeline returns.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/union-with-2" twoslash />

Now both parts have the same type, and there's no union left. The rows of the current pipeline come first, the appended ones after them.

## Walk a tree of links [#traverse-graph]

A category stores its parent's name, the parent stores its own parent, and so on. To find every ancestor of `Smartphones` without knowing how deep the tree goes, use [`graphLookup`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#graph-lookup). It repeats the search for as long as it finds new links.

- `startWith`: where to start.
- `connectFromField` and `connectToField`: how to get from a found document to the next one.
- `depthField`: where to record the step at which a document was found.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/traverse-graph" twoslash />

Don't rely on the order of the ancestors. In the example above the nearest one came first, but the server doesn't promise that. Sort by `level` instead. It's the step number, an int64. `maxDepth` limits the depth, and `restrictSearchWithMatch` filters the documents at every step.

## How to choose [#how-to-choose]

Ask yourself what your rows are missing.

- **Fields from another collection**: use `lookup`. The simple form when two fields only need to be equal, the sub-pipeline form when you need conditions or totals.
- **More rows, not more fields**: `unionWith`.
- **Links of unknown depth**: `graphLookup`.

## When not to use it [#when-not-to-use]

If you need documents of one collection with their related documents filled in, use [populate](../populate/index.mdx). It returns the related documents with their types and methods. A join in an aggregation is for when the output is a report, with grouping, totals, and a new row shape.

Keep the cost in mind, too. `lookup` runs for every row, so first filter and trim the rows with [`match`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#match) and [`limit`](../reference/aggregation/stages.mdx#limit), and only then join.

## Troubleshooting [#troubleshooting]

**A typo in a join field.** The compiler checks `localField` and `foreignField` against the models, so you see the mistake right away.

<Snippet id="aggregation/joins/troubleshooting" twoslash />

Change the name to `customer`.

**A row disappears after `unwind`.** After `lookup` the order from `dave` was still there, and after `unwind` it's gone. That's the server's behavior, because unwinding an empty array produces no rows. Add `preserveNullAndEmptyArrays: true`, or don't unwind the array.

**A field of the second collection is out of reach after `unionWith`.** The row type is now a union, and you can't read a field that one of the parts lacks without checking first. Bring both parts to one shape with `project` in a sub-pipeline, as in the example above.
