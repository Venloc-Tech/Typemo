---
title: What data policies are
description: "Which rules the model applies to every operation on its own, and why you can't turn them off for a single query"
icon: Shield
---

A **policy** is a rule the model applies to each of its operations on its own, without the query's author taking part. So you can't forget it and you can't turn it off. You turn a policy on once in the schema, and it applies to reads, writes, aggregations, related data, and change streams.

Policies protect you from forgetfulness, not from malice. Forgot the organization condition, forgot to exclude deleted records, forgot to record who changed the data? The policy does it for you.

Typemo has two kinds of policies, and the difference matters. You turn on the **app policies** yourself.

- [`tenant`](../reference/policies/schema-options.mdx#tenant) keeps the data of different organizations in one collection. The tenant field is marked with the [`@Tenant()`](../reference/schemas/field-markers.mdx#tenant) decorator.
- [`softDelete`](../reference/policies/schema-options.mdx#soft-delete) sets a mark on delete instead of erasing the document.
- [`audit`](../reference/policies/schema-options.mdx#audit) leaves a trace of every write.

**Strictness policies** always work and can't be turned off. They catch an unknown field, an empty filter in a write (including `updateOne` and `deleteOne`), an operator inside data, `undefined`, and a `limit` that isn't positive. The general idea is described on [Strictness](../concepts/strictness.mdx).

## How policies see an operation [#how-policies-see-operation]

Policies don't read flags in the query. Everything they need (the tenant, the actor, whether to show deleted documents) lives in the **policy context** and arrives explicitly. It comes from one of two places: a scope opened by [`PolicyContext.run`](../reference/policies/policy-context.mdx#policy-context.run), or [`.policy()`](../reference/query/options.mdx#policy) on a single operation. Say a service takes the organization from the HTTP request and opens a scope once. Every model call inside it works with that organization. There's more in [Pass the tenant and actor through the whole request](./policy-context.mdx).

Every operation goes through the same steps, and the policies come before the request is sent to the server. So a violation is caught before the network. If a policy can't run the operation safely, the operation goes nowhere, and you get a [`StrictModeError`](../reference/policies/violations.mdx#strict-mode-error) with the reason. The texts and reasons of every violation are in the [Policy violations](../reference/policies/violations.mdx) reference.

## Which policy to choose [#choose-policy]

Ask yourself **what must be impossible to do by mistake**.

- **Someone else's data must never get into a response or a write**: turn on `tenant`.
- **A delete must be reversible**: `softDelete`.
- **You need to prove who changed what**: `audit`.

These three policies are independent and combine well. A model with `tenant`, `softDelete`, and `audit` at once is common for an organization's documents. You don't choose strictness policies. They are always on.

## What policies don't do [#policy-limits]

Policies don't check access rights. They split data, but they don't decide who may run which operation. They don't replace validating client input either. For that there's [`untrusted`](../reference/policies/soft-delete-and-untrusted.mdx#untrusted) and schema validation.

And they don't relax. There's no option that would allow an unknown path or an empty filter "only this once". If you really need an operation that bypasses the rules, use [`client.unsafeDriver()`](../reference/connection/typemo-client.mdx#client.unsafe-driver). Then at least the bypass is visible in the code.

<Callout type="migration">

The Mongoose core has no such rules. Tenants and soft delete come from plugins and `pre` hooks, and those are easy to bypass: an `updateOne` without the hook, a `lean()` without the filter. `strictQuery` can even be turned off. In Typemo, policies are built into the operation pipeline, and they have no "relaxed" mode.

</Callout>
